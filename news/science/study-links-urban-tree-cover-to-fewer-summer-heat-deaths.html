Study links urban tree cover to fewer summer heat deaths

Researchers at the Valmora Institute compared 90 city districts over a decade of heatwaves.

Sara Haddad

31 October 2026 at 05:00 UTC · 1 min read

Share

Neighbourhoods with more than 30% tree canopy recorded markedly fewer deaths during heatwaves than comparable districts with little greenery, according to a decade-long study published on Friday by the Valmora Institute.

The researchers analysed mortality, temperature and satellite canopy data from 90 districts across six cities between 2015 and 2025, adjusting for income, age and building type.

Key findings

  • Surface temperatures in well-shaded districts were on average 3–4°C lower on the hottest days.
  • Excess mortality during heatwaves was roughly a third lower in high-canopy districts.
  • The protective effect was strongest among residents over 75 and in districts with older housing stock.

Trees are infrastructure. They just happen to be alive.

— Study co-author

The authors caution that the study shows a strong association rather than definitive proof of cause, and that trees take decades to mature. They recommend cities combine planting with shorter-term measures such as cool roofs and public cooling centres.

Several city councils have already cited the findings in budget debates. The study's full dataset has been released openly to allow other researchers to test the results.
